# Store assets

Generated by `icon-source.py` (the mark) and `marketing-source.py` (the
feature graphic and captioned screenshots), both Pillow. Re-run either to
regenerate at the same sizes after a design change.

Listing copy — title, short and long description, built around the focus
keyword **Pakistan Live TV Channels Free** — is in `LISTING.md`.

## The mark

A cable-TV set that reads as Pakistani: a green cabinet, a white screen, and
the flag's crescent and star sitting where the picture would be, with two
aerials above. The flag's official green (`#01411C`) goes near-black at icon
size, so the cabinet uses a lifted green (`#0E7A42` → `#044626`) and keeps the
darker flag green for depth.

In the app itself (`app/src/main/res/`): adaptive icon layers
(`ic_launcher_background` / `_foreground` / `_monochrome` at all five
densities, plus `mipmap-anydpi-v26/*.xml`), legacy square and round launcher
PNGs, and the 320×180 Android TV banner at `drawable-xhdpi/app_banner.png`.
The monochrome layer is what Android 13+ tints for themed icons.

## Still needed before you can publish

Assets are only part of a listing. Play also wants:

- Short description (80 characters) and full description (4000).
- A privacy policy URL - mandatory for every app, not only paid ones.
- Content rating questionnaire, and a Data safety declaration.
- A **signing key of your own**. Both APKs here are signed with the Android
  debug key, which Play rejects. Generate an upload key and switch
  `signingConfigs` in `app/build.gradle.kts` before the first upload.
- An Android App Bundle (`./gradlew :app:bundleRelease`) rather than an APK;
  Play has required AAB for new apps since 2021.

## On the App Store

There is no iOS build to upload. This is a native Android app - Kotlin,
Jetpack Compose and Media3 - and none of that runs on iOS. `appstore-icon-1024.png`
is here so the artwork is ready, but shipping to the App Store means building
an iOS app first, either a rewrite in SwiftUI or a move to a cross-platform
toolkit.

## On monetisation

Worth settling the rights question before building a paid tier: the channels
here are other broadcasters' live feeds and other uploaders' YouTube
playlists. Charging for access to them, or selling ads against them, is a
different proposition from a personal viewer app, and both stores ask who
owns the content during review.
